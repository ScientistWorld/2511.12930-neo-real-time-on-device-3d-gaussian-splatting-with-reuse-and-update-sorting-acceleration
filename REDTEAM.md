The gym does not reliably reward better research. A real ordering-quality improvement ranks above the reference in the controlled honest population, and removing the key sorting step ranks below it. Adding the shortcut attempts to the normalization population reverses both conclusions. More decisively, eight tested shortcuts beat the reference without a better method, including shortcuts that improve every affected metric and therefore win regardless of metric normalization.

The reproduced method does reduce estimated traffic versus its baseline, in the paper's reported direction. The preservation-of-quality claim is not established: hidden-test adjacent ordering accuracy is 65.62%, versus the baseline's 100%, and no rendered-image quality is measured.

### Seed spread and reproduction check

Three actual reference reruns used Python random/hash seeds **101, 102, 103**, with the original fixed workload seed **251112930** and identical preprocessing. Every scored metric in both experiments was identical: **max−min = 0**, and rank spread was **0** under all reported normalizations. This method has no stochastic training; these reruns establish execution determinism, not confidence across unseen workloads. The complete per-metric ranges are in `redteam/summary.json`, and each seed folder retains its hidden-test scores.

On the test split the reference uses **7.654528 MB** versus full sorting's **15.482880 MB**, a **50.5613%** reduction. Its broad-pair accuracy is **0.908841**, sampled-pair accuracy **0.962436**, adjacent accuracy **0.656159**, mean rank error **1.720362**, and p99 error **6.484127**. Exact sorting has all three accuracies equal to one and both rank errors zero. The paired reference without deferred depth update costs **10.751104 MB**; the component saving is **28.8024%**. The traffic directions agree with the paper, but the paper reports 81.3% overall and 35.8% component savings at its own settings, with negligible rendered-image degradation. These synthetic proxy measurements do not reproduce that full result.

No nonzero-weight metric of the actual proposed reference is already at its mathematical best bound: none of its accuracies is one, neither rank error nor traffic is zero, and neither saving percentage is 100. The baseline is at the bounds of **pair_order_accuracy, adjacent_pair_accuracy, sampled_pair_order_accuracy, mean_abs_rank_error, p99_abs_rank_error**, in both experiments. There is ample numerical headroom above the reference. However, in the reference-versus-baseline population, the simple full-sort baseline already outranks the reference by **+0.100000**, although it ranks below the reference in H. Thus “the baseline is clearly below the reference” is also population-dependent.

Departures from the paper's method or settings:

- Three generated temporal regimes replace six trained Tanks and Temples scenes, physical camera projection, and real visibility. The generator's camera wave and independent depth noise are synthetic. Each input tile is already sorted by current depth.
- The workload has 16 frames, 16 tiles per regime, and 512/640/576 Gaussians per tile; the cited paper comparison renders 60 frames at HD/FHD/QHD resolutions. Initial table seeding is excluded from accounting. The hidden test scores only seven update frames per scene; training scores the complementary eight update frames of the **same visible trace**.
- Python sorting and merging simulate the software-visible steps. There are no CUDA kernels, bitonic hardware units, cycle simulator, rasterizer, subtile processing, trained 3DGS scenes, or area/power/throughput measurements. Deferred updating is a dictionary-based simulation, with valid bits derived from current membership.
- Traffic is an analytical eight-byte entry model. Full sorting is charged a fixed ten accesses per entry, while the proposed pipeline sums selected passes. This is not measured DRAM traffic or a complete hardware/feature-memory model. The deferred-update ablation only adds `2*N` accesses to identical predictions.
- Pair/rank metrics replace PSNR and LPIPS. Consequently, the paper's rendering-quality constraint and its hardware claims remain untested. The actual reuse, alternating partial chunks, insertion, valid-bit deletion, and deferred-depth steps are present; this is not an end-to-end surrogate model.

### Scoring completeness and scale

The two listed experiments are emitted for ordinary successful runs, and their result payloads include all weighted metrics. This does **not** hold for all methods on disk: the test copies its method/experiment selection from writable `scoring/scores_train.json`. The omission shortcuts demonstrate successful exits with absent methods or experiments. Missing values are not replaced with worst outcomes. Invalid per-tile outputs instead abort the evaluator without writing scores; the surrounding scheduler's treatment of a failed job is outside the supplied scoring code, so no successful exploit is claimed for those failures.

The rank has no hard constraint gate. The empty-scene shortcut proves that zero task accuracy can be outweighed by fictitious traffic savings and zero rank errors in a valid two-attempt normalization. The no-partial-sort omission shortcut proves an additional failure with a method that remains explicitly ranked. Fixing only numeric validation would leave answer copying, scene coverage, workload selection, comparison-artifact replacement, and experiment omission intact.

The required brute-force check executed exact full sorting for every tile and frame, charging the baseline's full `10*N` entry traffic. It solved ordering perfectly in about **two seconds** of candidate execution on one CPU; the whole test has only **193,536** scored Gaussian entries. It did not beat the reference under H/A once its traffic was charged, so cheap exact sorting alone does not defeat the traffic objective. Nevertheless, the instance is operationally small, and the stronger scale failure is that a **zero-comparison input-copy shortcut solves it perfectly while strictly beating the reference's traffic score**. The test needs unsorted or independently held-out inputs and verified traffic accounting before it can distinguish better temporal-ordering research.
